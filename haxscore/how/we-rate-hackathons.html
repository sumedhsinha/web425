Hackathon Review Methodology

How We Evaluate Events

Version 1.0 (Current)

Hackathons are high-pressure environments where logistics, mentorship, and community quality matter just as much as the venue name. A good event on paper can still fail attendees if Wi-Fi is unstable, judging is unclear, or mentorship is inaccessible.

Because of that, HaxScore reviews events using a multi-category rubric built around real attendee outcomes: can participants learn, build, ship, and leave with momentum?

1. Evidence Collection Process

Each hackathon review is based on first-hand attendance plus documented evidence. We collect notes during the event window, verify details post-event, and cross-check organizer claims against what attendees actually experienced.

  • Primary evidence: first-hand observations, photos, schedule tracking, and category-by-category field notes.
  • Secondary evidence: official event pages, Devpost galleries, MLH listings, published schedules, and sponsor announcements.
  • Community signal: participant sentiment from public channels (e.g., Discord/Slack threads when available), especially for recurring pain points.

2. Core Scoring Categories

Every event receives category ratings on a 5-star scale. These are the same dimensions used in our review sidebar scorecards:

  • Food & Accommodations: meal quality/frequency, dietary inclusivity, sleep space practicality, and hydration/snack availability.
  • Hardware & APIs: availability and quality of hardware checkout, API/documentation support, and sponsor tooling usefulness.
  • Logistics & Venue: check-in flow, schedule clarity, venue layout, power/outlet access, Wi-Fi reliability, and overnight usability.
  • Mentorship Quality: mentor availability, technical depth, response times, and usefulness for beginner vs advanced teams.
  • Community & Sponsors: inclusivity, collaborative culture, sponsor engagement quality, and signal-to-noise ratio of sponsor activity.

3. Overall Rating Model

Category scores are combined into an overall event rating. In v1.0 we use weighted scoring so infrastructure and educational value have stronger influence than hype factors.

  • Logistics & Venue: 25%
  • Mentorship Quality: 25%
  • Hardware & APIs: 20%
  • Community & Sponsors: 20%
  • Food & Accommodations: 10%

Weights may evolve in later versions, but all methodology changes are versioned on this page and applied only to future reviews unless explicitly restated.

4. Fairness, Transparency, and Bias Controls

  • No paid rankings: sponsorships and organizer relationships do not influence scores.
  • Disclosure policy: if travel, lodging, or registration is subsidized, it is disclosed in the review.
  • Evidence threshold: strong claims require direct observation or verifiable public evidence.
  • No recency inflation: one great keynote or flashy demo cannot outweigh systemic issues like poor Wi-Fi or unclear judging.

5. Interpreting the Star Scale

  • 5.0: Category-leading execution; exceptional for most participants.
  • 4.0 - 4.5: Strong event with minor operational gaps.
  • 3.0 - 3.5: Mixed experience; workable but notable friction points.
  • 2.0 - 2.5: Repeated issues likely to hinder learning/shipping outcomes.
  • Below 2.0: Significant organizational failures requiring major improvement.

6. Versioning and Update Policy

Methodology versions are tagged (v1.0, v1.1, etc.) and linked from each review sidebar. If we revise criteria, the new version is published here before it is used in future reviews.
